## Goal

Implement a playable strategic ruleset in which factions own provinces and
command independent armies. The player can raise a second army, station and
withdraw troops, defend a province, lose territory and take it back. Strategic
opponents use the same action rules as the player. Use the existing campaign
as an integration test and playable entry point. The deliverable is a reusable
system driven by a campaign definition. It must run on a different authored
graph and army setup without changing engine code.

Read AGENTS.md, docs/WORK_ALLOCATION.md, DELIB-0008, CX-028 and CX-032. Inspect
StrategicCampaign, CampaignRoster, CampaignEncounter, the checkpoint store and
the strategic UI. Reuse the roster, construction catalogue, battle session,
atomic save publication and existing UI conventions where their contracts fit.

## Strategic authority and turns

- Model stable faction, province, army and unit identities independently.
  A province has one owner (or is neutral) and an optional garrison. Each
  army has a faction, position, roster and remaining move budget. A living
  unit belongs to exactly one army or garrison. Record casualties separately
  from deployable units and retain their identity. Validate authored graphs,
  home/objective references, opening ownership, armies and deployment capacity.
  Derive ownership and location from authoritative state. UI selection is
  separate from model state and costs no action.
- New contested mode uses explicit **End Turn**. Each army can travel one
  adjacent edge per player round, and all armies get their budget back on
  the next player round. A capture consumes the moving army's edge budget.
  Permit one paid service per faction round at its own Home (build, recruit
  or recover), priced before income. Reuse CX-032 prerequisites and effects.
  A new Market contributes beginning with the following round's payout. Transfers and
  formation/disbanding spend no gold or round, and cannot refresh movement.
  Credit each faction's income exactly once per completed round. Document
  the exact transition order and exercise the last-round victory boundary.
- Owned provinces can hold a garrison plus at most one friendly field army.
  At a friendly location transfer selected living troops between the stationed
  army and garrison. Track movement eligibility across transfers so a troop
  cannot move twice in one round by switching armies. Raise a new army at a friendly province with a garrison
  by transferring troops to it, subject to the field-army limit. Newly raised
  armies start without a move that round. An empty army disbands. Transfers
  require co-location and respect limits; they cannot duplicate troops,
  heal wounds, transfer casualties, or move units through a pending battle.
  Recruitment feeds the Home garrison, and recovery can target eligible
  co-located friendly troops. Keep roster caps, budgets, prices, stats,
  income and round limits authored. A small cap such as two field armies per
  faction is sufficient for the first playable campaign.

## Contests and rival turns

- Movement into undefended nonfriendly territory changes control. Hostile
  occupation, garrison or both trigger a real tactical encounter. Deploy
  a deterministic combined defense up to the authored tactical capacity.
  Reject a configuration that can strand a legal army/garrison combination
  without a supported deployment. Preserve actual definitions, life and
  identities for participants on both sides. Route result collection through
  the existing detached encounter boundary or a focused extension of it.
  Resolve both rosters and the control change atomically exactly once. Define
  the post-battle position and garrison of each survivor for both outcomes,
  including when a defending army survives. Losing
  a field army is survivable while the faction still controls its capital.
  Losing the capital or exhausting the round limit ends the player run.
  Home and its buildings stay tied to the province.
- A player-initiated attack may be abandoned without advancing the round,
  transferring ownership or applying casualties; preserve existing retry and
  stale-result protections. A rival attack on an Azure army or garrison
  suspends the rival phase for an interactive defense. The player can defend
  or explicitly concede. Concession follows a documented loss outcome and
  cannot cancel the rival turn. Resume the remaining rival phase exactly once
  after resolution. Make tactical side selection and attacker initiative
  work for defense as well as attack. Use existing tactical commands and
  AI, without scripted damage or fabricated winners.
- Rival policy selects legal moves from the board using visible state.
  Prioritize capturable enemy territory or defended objectives by authored
  policy weights and use a stable tie-break. Allow holding, capture, and
  reinforcement via the same formation, movement and service rules. No
  scripted lanes, province-ID special cases or per-turn event script.
  Policy has a bounded number of commands and consumes no tactical RNG.
  Use a deterministic seed for encounters through the current seed boundary.
