## Map and battle reference details

The supplied 638×480 map still and 1048×822 combat still make the status and
navigation hierarchy clearer. Approximate proportions below are measured
inside the game client area. Use them as layout references and tune for the
supported widescreen sizes.

- **Strategic map.** The resource/navigation strip spans the full bottom edge
  (roughly 6% of client height in the still). It groups currency at the left,
  contextual province text centrally and compact navigation at the right.
  The framed portrait/status card is a separate region above the strip. Its
  identity, small icon/value rows, objective text and action slots are distinct.
  Keep the strip visible while inspecting a province or switching to Home.
  Show the inspected province name, ownership, and income/availability when
  the model provides them. Keep the army's actual location distinct from
  inspected and actionable destinations. Put Map and Home navigation in
  consistent places, with an active state. Use existing roster status in the
  card when inspecting a member. Do not infer hero stats, inventory, quests
  or an End Turn command from the screenshot.
- **Combat.** The framed right rail is about 14% of the reference client width.
  Use a compact rail as the visual target and widen it where EGO's labels or
  hit areas need room. Stack portrait and primary vitals, aligned secondary
  stats, a two-column action grid, a distinct effects region, and a persistent
  footer. Keep end/pass and cancel discoverable, plus Skip during playback.
  Use a labeled detail view for logs, descriptions and auxiliary settings.
  Show which side is active, which unit is selected or being inspected, and
  which input mode is armed. The reference has visible per-unit bars and
  selection rings directly on the field. Preserve life and selection there,
  show stamina when the presentation has an authoritative value (including
  intermediate playback snapshots), and omit any additional inferred bar.
  An unavailable action must never hide or impersonate a live action.
- **Icon navigation.** Inspect neighboring button states and native dimensions
  before cropping. Name each live control for its EGO action, give icon-only
  buttons readable help on focus and hover, and keep clear active/pressed,
  disabled and focus states. Treat unknown reference glyphs as visual evidence
  of layout only. Empty cells remain inert and keyboard traversal reaches
  live controls in screen order.

## Implementation

1. **Menu.** Allocate roughly 60–65% of game viewport height to the unobstructed
   panorama. Keep the title near top center. Build the continuous lower frame
   with narrow, uniform parchment rows. Aim for 24–30% of viewport width
   at 1152x648 and 1440x810, with enough width for the longest label and
   comfortable hit areas at 960x540. Avoid the nested opaque center box and
   extra space beneath the choices. Crop the art proportionally and preserve
   pan direction, center stop, stationary hitboxes and visible keyboard focus.
   Keep all three playable entries, Controls, About and Quit reachable.
   Controls and About may share a compact utility row or live in Options. Group
   the existing mute/volume controls under a working Options entry. Keep a
   disabled Multiplayer row with its availability exposed in text or nearby
   help. Use the existing art resolver and authored fallback.
2. **Province map and Home.** Give the map or settlement art the largest
   continuous area. Put gold, turn and the current objective in a compact
   bottom strip. Use one right-hand context panel for province inspection or
   company/Home services, with active selection and legal travel obvious.
   Keep new/resume/save/menu reachable via a clearly named campaign control
   or dedicated section. Preserve the graph, link geometry/hit alignment,
   reachable and current province cues, checkpoint behavior, capture feedback,
   roster inspection, recovery and recruitment. Keep Home's existing cost
   and availability explanations close to the actions. Reserve a compact
   slot for construction in Home and a crystal slot alongside map gold if
   they fit the reference composition. Show construction as unavailable and
   crystals as an em dash with a label. There is no authored value for either.
3. **Battle.** Use the reference's field-first layout. Compact portrait,
   life/stamina/movement, attack/defence/ammo and effects into one scannable
   framed rail. Keep essential commands and Skip reachable at minimum size
   during play, and keep long logs and descriptions accessible in a
   scrollable detail region or a separate disclosure. Style the playable
   hex area and surrounding terrain as one field while keeping highlights
   legible and click geometry intact. Add a compact action picker inspired by
   the reference slot-and-description pattern. Surface the current unit's
   real granted action (Stamina Drain in the demo where applicable) through
   the existing legality and targeting path. Preserve direct controls and
   shortcuts, right information during targeting, and cancel/return focus.
   Empty action slots may complete the grid. Give the spell slot a plainly
   unavailable state. Preserve Normal/Fast/Instant, playback ordering, audio
   controls and reduced motion. Use the existing typed observations and
   command routing.
4. **Availability.** Use live values and working controls when the current
   model supports them. Render absent values as labeled em dashes and
   unavailable actions as visibly disabled, with a reason discoverable by
   keyboard and pointer. Keep blank decorative slots inert and out of tab
   order. Distinguish unsupported features from real commands that are
   temporarily illegal. No fake zeros, fake resources, fake progress, empty
   pages behind enabled buttons, or state changes from a placeholder. Do
   not add further feature slots simply to fill space.

Keep clean separation between scene composition and authoritative state.
Existing local/partial/public art behavior must continue to work. Reuse
existing themed control states and mapping keys. Keep extracted assets,
footage and captures private. Scope includes game UI, authored fallbacks
and capture tooling, targeted tests, and this task's documentation.
Gameplay mechanics, campaign expansion, full original UI replication,
new production sound mapping and checkpoint format changes are outside scope.
